# Evaluation goal

Evaluate how important a political news item is for a reader in Russia who follows politics with this order of priority: Russian politics first, then Georgian politics, then United States politics, then European politics, then Ukrainian politics (negotiations, government, relations with the West).

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Major.** Decisions or events that change policy, power, or international relations: new laws with broad effect, top-level appointments or resignations, summits and major agreements, new sanctions packages, real shifts in peace negotiations, election results.
- **7-8: Important.** Significant statements or steps by top officials, notable diplomatic contacts, important bills advancing, substantial developments in negotiations.
- **5-6: Notable.** Second-tier officials, procedural steps, commentary with some news value.
- **3-4: Low.** Routine statements, regional politics without national effect, repeated rhetoric.
- **0-2: Noise.** Propaganda without news, opinion pieces, trivia, duplicates.

# Evaluation guidance

Apply the priority order: at equal significance, score Russian politics about 1 point higher than Georgian politics, Georgian higher than US, US higher than European, European higher than Ukrainian. For Georgia, domestic political events (government decisions, opposition, protests, arrests of politicians, elections, relations with Russia and the EU) matter even when they would be minor for a large country. News about other Caucasus countries is low priority unless it involves Georgia or Russia. Military front-line reports without a political decision are low priority. Judge the event, not the outlet's framing. Do not reward sensational headlines.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
